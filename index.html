FICAM Overview

Federal Identity, Credential, and Access Management, FICAM, is the United States federal government;s implementation of Identity, Credential, and Access Management (ICAM).

ICAM is the set of tools, policies, and information systems that an agency uses to establish the right individual to access the right resource, at the right time, for the right reason in support of federal business objectives.

Layered reference architecture

Modeled loosely on the OSI seven-layer discipline: each tier has a narrow job and a named standards body it answers to.

Application — forensics, photojournalism, insurance documentation, drone attestation.
Provenance envelope — the WHO / WHEN / WHERE / WHAT claim, packaged as a portable, verifiable record.
Standards anchor — ISO 19111, IEEE 1588, NIST SP 800-63, W3C Verifiable Credentials.
Authenticated edge SOC — CSAC + GNSS-RTK + image sensor, fused in hardware at capture.
Compute fabric — HPC + GPU tensors + QPU (reserved), on NLINK Cloud.

Smartphone as the authenticated edge

A consumer smartphone already fields most of the edge SOC in commodity form, ahead of any custom silicon:

Layer Smartphone component Standards anchor
WHO Secure Enclave / StrongBox + biometric sensor Apple App Attest, Android Play Integrity
WHEN System clock + NTP drift check IEEE 1588 (reference model)
WHERE Onboard GNSS (meter-level, not RTK) ISO 19111
WHAT Camera + secure-element-signed frame hash W3C Verifiable Credentials

Phone GNSS is meter-level, not RTK centimeter-level — a deliberate MVP tradeoff, not a final architecture.

Who governs each claim

No single authority runs Spacetime Positional Provenance. Each claim type answers to a different standards body; jurisdiction only enters when a claim is disputed.

Claim Standards body Enforcement
WHO NIST SP 800-63 Compulsory in-jurisdiction, treaty-bridged, voluntary beyond
WHEN IEEE 1588 Physics-anchored, not jurisdictional
WHERE ISO 19111 Physics-anchored, not jurisdictional
WHAT W3C Verifiable Credentials / JSON-LD Cryptographic, not jurisdictional

WHO — the three enforcement zones

Compulsory — within a jurisdiction, courts and regulators can compel disclosure or reject a claim below their assurance-level bar.
Treaty-bridged — between cooperating jurisdictions, mutual recognition and mutual legal assistance treaties extend a claim's weight, slowly.
Voluntary — everywhere else. No compulsory force exists; a well-designed cryptographic envelope lets a verifier trust the math even absent a treaty.

EULA and app-install permission consent is what makes the voluntary zone usable: an affirmative grant of scope (camera, location, biometric match) stands in as a contractual basis for the WHO claim where law hasn't reached. This imports GDPR biometric-data provisions, Illinois BIPA and comparable state statutes, and each platform's attestation API terms — respected, not re-invented.

Schema v2 — GeoJSON, CRS, and enterprise identity

The envelope now carries WHERE and WHAT as a standard GeoJSON Feature, wrapped in a FeatureCollection, with explicit CRS metadata and an extended WHO block that supports enterprise device identity via AWS IAM / IoT Core alongside the original session-key stand-in.

Why GeoJSON + CRS

GeoJSON (RFC 7946) is the common interchange format for spatial data, which makes the envelope directly consumable by mapping tools, GIS pipelines, and the existing GNSS-RTK Assistant. RFC 7946 assumes WGS84 (CRS84) everywhere and formally deprecates embedding other CRS values — but real RTK deployments often reference a local or regional datum. This schema keeps crs as an explicit, documented foreign member for provenance and audit clarity, on the understanding that any consumer expecting strict RFC 7946 should reproject to CRS84 first.

Why AWS IAM / IoT Core in the WHO layer

Apple App Attest and Play Integrity cover consumer smartphones. Fleet and enterprise deployments — a fleet of drones, fixed forensic cameras, insurance inspection rigs — more often authenticate through AWS IoT Core device certificates (X.509) paired with an IAM role scoping what that device is authorized to attest to. The schema adds these as optional WHO fields alongside the session key, so the same envelope shape works for a phone in someone's pocket or a fleet of registered devices.

Full architecture note

      
Merged from the standalone GeoEdge_SpacetimePositionalProvenance document so
        everything lives in one file.


      
1. Concept

      
Spacetime Positional Provenance (SPP) extends the GeoEdge Precision Provenance wedge from a
        WHEN+WHERE claim into a full four-attribute forensic attestation: WHO generated the record,
        WHEN it occurred, WHERE it occurred, and WHAT was captured. The distinguishing move is where
        the truth is generated: not asserted after the fact in software, but produced at the point of
        capture by an authenticated edge System-on-Chip, then carried through a standards-anchored
        envelope so any downstream verifier — a court, an insurer, a newsroom — can validate
        it without trusting the party who submitted it.

      
This reframes the GeoEdge provenance line from a geolocation-metadata feature into a
        general-purpose chain-of-custody primitive, with the four forensic beachhead verticals
        (forensics, photojournalism, insurance documentation, drone attestation) as first customers
        rather than the whole addressable market.


      
2. Layered reference architecture

      
Modeled loosely on the OSI seven-layer discipline: each tier has a narrow job and a named
        standards body it answers to, so the stack can be audited layer by layer rather than taken on
        faith as a monolith. See the Architecture tab for the full table.


      
3. The authenticated edge SOC — the actual invention

      
Everything above the edge SOC layer is applications consuming a record; everything below it
        is commodity compute. The novel contribution is fusing three independent truths into one
        attested package at the silicon level: CSAC (timing truth, independent of spoofable network
        time), GNSS-RTK (centimeter-level positional truth), and an ISOCELL-class image sensor (visual
        truth, bound to the same timing and position source at capture, not stitched on afterward).
        Because all three are fused before the record ever leaves the device, a verifier is checking a
        hardware attestation rather than trusting a chain of software claims — the same trust
        model shift that moved payment cards from magnetic stripe to chip-and-PIN.


      
4. Compute tier — NLINK Cloud

      
HPC handles bulk provenance verification and batch record processing. GPU tensors handle
        vision inference and pose/motion work, continuous with the existing MoveNet pipeline. QPU is
        reserved as a forward slot for the MULTIVERSITY quantum-walk work, most plausibly as a hardware
        source of verifiable randomness or an unclonable challenge-response primitive for the identity
        layer, once that research matures enough to justify it.


      
5. Relationship to existing GeoEdge work

      
The Precision Provenance one-pager is the wedge SPP architecturally expands from
        WHEN+WHERE to WHO/WHEN/WHERE/WHAT. The GNSS-RTK Assistant supplies the positional truth
        component of the edge SOC. MULTIVERSITY is the candidate source for the QPU-reserved function
        in the compute tier. The C2 Flight Deck / Tactical Island work is a natural early consumer,
        with drone attestation as one of the four beachhead verticals.


      
6. Resolved — minimum viable edge SOC prototype path

      
The fusion demo does not require custom silicon and can be assembled almost entirely from
        existing GeoEdge components, following the standard single-file HTML5, no-build-tooling
        constraint. Position truth reuses the GNSS-RTK Assistant as-is (Browser Geolocation API plus
        WebSerial / MQTT / WiFi-TCP / Bluetooth BLE for real RTK receivers). Visual truth reuses the
        OpenCV.js webcam tab already built in that same app. Timing truth substitutes the CSAC with the
        PPS (pulse-per-second) signal most GNSS/RTK receivers already expose, disciplined to the same
        satellite atomic clocks a CSAC references, falling back to an NTP/PTP drift check where PPS
        isn't exposed. Fusion happens at capture: hash the video frame, pair it with the RTK
        coordinates and the verified timestamp, sign the bundle, and emit it as the provenance
        envelope. This is "SPP MVP Demo v1" — mostly assembly of already-shipped components plus
        one new fusion-and-signing layer.


      
7. Smartphone as the authenticated edge SOC

      
See the Architecture tab for the full component table: Secure Enclave / StrongBox for the
        hardware root of trust, biometric sensor bound to it for the device-side WHO layer, onboard
        GNSS for a lower-fidelity WHERE, system clock plus NTP for WHEN, and camera plus secure-element
        attestation for WHAT. Apple App Attest and Android Play Integrity serve as the standards anchor
        for this layer, the same role NIST, IEEE, ISO, and W3C play elsewhere in the stack.


      
8. WHO — jurisdiction, treaty, and the voluntary zone

      
See the Governance tab for the full breakdown of the three enforcement zones —
        compulsory within a jurisdiction, treaty-bridged between cooperating jurisdictions, and
        voluntary everywhere else — and the role of EULA/consent as the mechanism that makes the
        voluntary zone usable.


      
9. Schema v2 — GeoJSON, CRS, and enterprise identity

      
See the Schema & Registry tab for the full GeoJSON FeatureCollection schema, the CRS
        metadata rationale, the AWS IAM/IoT Core WHO-layer extension, and the local SOC CRUD registry
        that simulates a production device registry.
